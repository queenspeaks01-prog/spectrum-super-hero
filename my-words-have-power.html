⚡ My Super-Power Words

When words are hard to find, I can try one of these:

🛑 Stop, please.
🧘 I need a break.
🤝 Help me, please.
🔉 Too loud!
😠 I'm frustrated.
💜 I need space.
🔁 Can I try again?
👂 Please listen.

A child does not have to say these aloud. Pointing, gestures, sign language, and AAC are powerful communication too.

💙 Caregiver Guide: When Children Use Strong Words

Swearing or repeating upsetting words may be connected to frustration, imitation, sensory experiences, scripting, or difficulty communicating. The same word can serve different purposes for different children.

  • Stay calm: Avoid shaming, yelling, or turning the word into a power struggle.
  • Look for the message: Notice what happened before the language and what the child may be trying to express.
  • Model alternatives: “I’m mad,” “Stop please,” or “I need space.” Offer pictures, gestures, or AAC as needed.
  • Teach at a calm time: Practice through role-play, stories, or brief demonstrations rather than during distress.
  • Set kind, consistent boundaries: Explain that some words can hurt people while still acknowledging the child's feelings.
  • Notice helpful communication: Respond when a child asks for help or a break, even if the words are imperfect.
  • Seek support when needed: A speech-language pathologist or qualified clinician can help when communication challenges cause ongoing distress.

Important: Never withhold a needed break, communication aid, or comfort until a child uses the “right” words. Respect all safe forms of communication.
