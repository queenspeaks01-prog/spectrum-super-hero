🦸 My Independent Daily Checklist

John says: Your pace is your superpower. Help is always okay!

For older children and teens — choose the steps that fit your day. Help is always allowed.

Morning

  • Bathroom and personal care
  • Brush teeth and get dressed
  • Eat breakfast and pack what I need

After school

  • Take a break or regulate my senses
  • Check assignments or plans
  • Make time for interests, movement, and food

Evening

  • Prepare what I need for tomorrow
  • Wash up and brush teeth
  • Choose a relaxing activity before bed

Independence can include reminders, visuals, assistive technology, and help from trusted adults.

💙 Caregiver Guide: Make Routines Work for Your Child

  • Start with just two or three meaningful steps rather than introducing every routine at once.
  • Show the schedule before a transition. Point to “first” and “next” without using threats or withholding essential needs.
  • Offer meaningful choices about order, clothing, lighting, sound, or preferred activities.
  • Build in sensory and movement breaks. Avoid forcing eye contact or demanding verbal responses.
  • Use a check mark, sticker, or simple praise if the child enjoys it. Avoid punishing missed steps.
  • Adapt for communication needs, motor skills, age, school schedules, and energy levels.
  • If routines suddenly become difficult, consider illness, sleep changes, pain, stress, or unmet support needs.
