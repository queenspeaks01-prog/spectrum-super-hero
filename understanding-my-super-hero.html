Understanding My Super Hero

Because understanding comes before helping. Practical, judgment-free information for families of autistic children ages 2–12.

Different abilities. Stronger together.
Start here

Every autistic child is different. These behaviors do not occur in all autistic children, and they are not automatically signs of defiance. Consider comfort, communication, sensory needs, and medical causes before choosing a response.

🚽

Potty training & toilet refusal

Body signals, sensory discomfort, anxiety, and communication can affect toileting.

For ages 2–6

Use a predictable routine, stable foot support, picture steps, and praise for effort.

For ages 7–12

Offer private reminders, discuss school bathroom access, and support independent wiping and handwashing.

When to seek help

Ask a pediatrician about constipation, painful urination, frequent accidents, or a loss of skills.

🛁

Bathing & hygiene

Water, noise, temperature, soap smells, and hair washing may feel overwhelming.

For ages 2–6

Offer choices between washcloth and cup, use gentle products, and show a short picture routine.

For ages 7–12

Respect privacy; offer a written checklist, choices of products, and a gradual independence plan.

When to seek help

Seek care for skin pain, persistent irritation, or sudden distress around bathing.

💬

Cussing & communication

A child may repeat heard words, communicate frustration, seek reactions, or struggle with impulse control.

For ages 2–6

Respond calmly and model phrases like “I need help” or “I am upset.”

For ages 7–12

Discuss where language is appropriate, notice triggers, and teach alternative ways to express strong feelings.

When to seek help

Ask for professional support if speech changes suddenly or the behavior causes major distress.

🧼

Stool withholding & fecal smearing

Constipation, discomfort, sensory exploration, difficulty cleaning, or communication needs may contribute. No single cause fits every child.

For ages 2–6

Check for constipation and discomfort; supervise hygiene and use simple bathroom pictures.

For ages 7–12

Offer discreet assistance and privacy, teach a cleanup routine, and explore patterns without shame.

When to seek help

Contact a pediatrician for repeated smearing, constipation, leakage, pain, or sudden changes; severe abdominal pain or vomiting needs urgent assessment.

Helpful next steps

Observe what happens before and after a challenge, use calm language, offer choices, and ask the child how they feel in a way they can communicate. A pediatrician can assess medical causes; occupational therapists and speech-language pathologists may help with daily routines and communication.

Safety: Wash hands and contaminated surfaces thoroughly after stool contact. Seek prompt medical advice for blood in stool, severe pain, vomiting, or signs of dehydration.

Educational information only; not a substitute for individualized medical care.
